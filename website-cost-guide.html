How much does a website cost in 2026?

A website costs from about $300 for a simple landing page to as much as $120,000 for a large web app in 2026, according to Project Cost Estimator. The same source puts a business website at $800 to $10,000, with about $4,000 as typical. At Fluidweb a website starts at $1,450, and a small business site with custom design, a CMS and SEO is $7,730.

By Wissile Sogoyou·Last updated November 17, 2026

What do published price ranges say?

Two public sources give a sense of the market. Project Cost Estimator lists a range for each kind of site, and WebFX compares what freelancers and agencies charge for web design.

Why are the ranges so wide?

"A website" can mean one page with a contact form or a product with accounts, payments and a database. The price follows the scope: how many pages there are, whether each page is designed from scratch, and how much the site has to do beyond showing content. A range tells you what kind of budget is realistic. An itemized list tells you what your own project costs.

Does a freelancer cost less than an agency?

Usually, but the ranges overlap. WebFX puts web design by a freelancer at roughly $500 to $10,000+ and by an agency at roughly $3,000 to $30,000+. With a freelancer you deal with the person doing the work. An agency brings a team. Fluidweb is a one-person studio, so its prices sit in the freelancer range for most projects.

What do you pay after launch?

Every build includes 30 days of support after launch for bug fixes. After that a care plan is optional: $300 for 3 months or $1,000 for 12 months, prepaid, with no automatic renewal. The plan covers updates, backups, monitoring and two hours of changes each month. Changes to the scope are priced with the same blocks as the original build.
