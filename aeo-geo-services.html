What it is

What AEO and GEO covers

AEO (answer engine optimization) and GEO (generative engine optimization) are about being understood, quoted and linked by AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI overviews. These tools reply to a question with one written answer instead of a list of links.

The work overlaps with SEO and runs alongside it. What matters most, in order: content that answers the question in its first sentences, HTML a crawler can read without running JavaScript, structured data, a clear picture of who you are and what you offer (entity clarity), and mentions of you on other sites. The first four are built into your pages by this block. Mentions on other sites are earned outside your site and are not part of it.

Two things are worth saying plainly. Google no longer shows FAQ rich results (since May 2026), so FAQ markup is not a shortcut to more space in search results. And llms.txt, a summary file for AI tools, is unproven: no major AI platform has confirmed that it reads the file. Nobody can promise that an assistant will cite a given site.

Questions

AEO and GEO questions

What is the difference between SEO, AEO and GEO?

SEO helps pages appear in search results. AEO (answer engine optimization) helps content get picked as the direct answer to a question. GEO (generative engine optimization) helps content get used and cited when an AI assistant writes an answer. The three share most of their groundwork.

How much do AEO and GEO cost?

AEO / GEO optimization costs $50 per page, added to a website build. On an eight-page site that is $400.

Can you guarantee that ChatGPT or Perplexity will cite my site?

No. Each assistant decides what it reads and cites, and nobody outside those companies controls it. The block covers what is in the site's control: answer-first content, crawlable HTML, structured data and clear entity signals.

Does an llms.txt file help?

It is unproven. The file is cheap to publish, but no major AI platform has confirmed that it reads it, so it should not be the reason to expect citations.

Do FAQ rich results still appear in Google?

No. Google no longer shows FAQ rich results (since May 2026). Clear questions and answers on a page are still useful to readers and to AI assistants, but the markup no longer earns extra space in search results.
