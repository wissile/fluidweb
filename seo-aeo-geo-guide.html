SEO, AEO and GEO: what each one is and what it costs

SEO helps your pages appear in search results. AEO and GEO help AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI overviews understand, quote and link to your content. At Fluidweb SEO optimization costs $50 per page and AEO / GEO optimization costs $50 per page, so both together add $800 to an eight-page site.

By Wissile Sogoyou·Last updated November 17, 2026

What is SEO?

SEO stands for search engine optimization. It is the work that helps a search engine find your pages, understand what they are about and show them for the right searches. On the site itself that means accurate page titles and descriptions, clear headings, structured data, a sitemap, clean addresses and pages that load fast.

What is AEO?

AEO stands for answer engine optimization. An answer engine replies to a question with one answer instead of a list of links. AEO is writing and structuring a page so that its answer is easy to lift out: the question is stated, and the answer comes first, in plain words.

What is GEO?

GEO stands for generative engine optimization. A generative engine, such as ChatGPT or Perplexity, writes a new answer from several sources and may cite them. GEO is making your content easy for those systems to read, trust and attribute to you.

How do SEO, AEO and GEO differ?

How SEO, AEO and GEO differ
TermAims forWhat success looks like
SEOA place in search resultsYour page is listed for a search and people click through

The three share most of their groundwork. A page that is clear, crawlable and well structured serves all of them.

What helps a site get cited by AI assistants?

In rough order of importance:

  1. Answer-first content. State the question, then answer it in the first sentence or two. Details follow.
  2. Crawlable HTML. The words and prices have to be in the page itself. Many AI crawlers do not run JavaScript, so content that only appears after a script runs may never be read.
  3. Structured data. Markup that states plainly what a page is: an article and its author, a service and its price, an organization and where it is.
  4. Entity clarity. One consistent description of who you are, what you offer, for whom and where, repeated the same way across the site.
  5. Third-party mentions. Other sites that name you and link to you. These are earned outside your own site and cannot be built into it.

What does not work, or is unproven?

  • FAQ rich results. Google no longer shows them (since May 2026). Questions and answers on a page still help readers and AI assistants, but FAQ markup does not earn extra space in search results any more.
  • llms.txt. A plain-text summary of a site for AI tools. It is cheap to publish, but unproven: no major AI platform has confirmed that it reads the file.
  • Guarantees. Nobody outside a search engine or an AI company controls what it ranks or cites. Anyone who promises a ranking or a citation is promising something they do not control.

Do you need all three?

Start with SEO. It is the base the other two stand on: if a search engine cannot read and understand a page, an AI assistant will not either. Add AEO and GEO when your customers are likely to ask an assistant the kind of question your site answers. A business with local customers should also consider local SEO ($250), which covers google Business Profile, local business schema and directory listings.
